Security assessment and staff training

Cybersecurity for a human-rights archive

I worked with a digital archive that collects and preserves evidence of human-rights violations in conflict regions.

I assessed security risks, developed recommendations, and created training for staff. My role did not include implementing the technical changes.

Background

Preserving evidence and protecting investigators

The archive collected and indexed material posted online so that evidence of human-rights violations could be preserved if the original material was removed, censored, or destroyed. Its work supported investigations by international organizations.

The organization faced threats from groups seeking to destroy evidence or intimidate investigators. The assessment covered its work across four conflict zones on three continents.

My work

Assessment, recommendations, and training

A

Risk assessment and threat matrix

I identified the threat actors that posed the greatest risk, the types of attacks the organization could face, and the resources most at risk. This included state and state-sponsored actors.

B

Staff training and guides

I developed training and guides on web browser security, password management, and safer file sharing. I also created self-assessment worksheets to help staff identify vulnerabilities in their own practices.

C

Tools and security review plans

I assessed vulnerabilities in the organization’s proprietary tools and developed plans for regular security audits, penetration testing, and network scanning.

Deliverables

A risk assessment, staff materials, and security plans

My work gave the organization an assessment of its risks, recommendations for its tools and processes, and materials for staff training. Implementation of the technical changes was outside my role.

The client’s identity, detailed findings, and security documents aren’t public. This overview describes the scope of my contribution.
